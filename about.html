Methods & Approach

The development of this open-source framework is centered on a single inquiry: What do we need to learn to intervene in complex systems? The methodology bridges established scholarship with localized, field-based practice by combining AI-assisted architecture, collective peer validation, and an immediate open-source release for continuous community expansion.

Structural Synthesis & AI-Assisted Architecture

The structural backbone of the 24 islands was developed by compiling an index of existing theoretical models, operational tools, and pedagogical frameworks. This foundational research matrix includes contributions from international systems change institutes, academic literature across disciplines (such as earth system science, decolonial thought, and complexity theory), and personal field practice.

To process this high-density input, large language models (Gemini and Claude) were engaged as structured thinking partners. The models functioned as technical design assistants, executing specific synthesis tasks under strict author guidance:

  • Taxonomical Consistency: Ensuring structural uniformity across all 24 islands so that each is explicitly unpacked through a three-part architecture: Definition, Learning Goals, and Resource Library.
  • Synthesis and Definition: Condensing diverse academic traditions, personal practice, and operational tools into a singular, cohesive focus to establish clear boundaries for each island.
  • Progressive Scaffolding: Translating raw framework data and conceptual inputs into a longitudinal 15-year developmental progression, mapping out structured milestones across four distinct proficiency levels: Emerging, Developing, Effective, and Influential.

Final editorial control, conceptual framing, and ethical parameters remained strictly with the primary authors. The AI tools did not replace original evaluation or judgment, but served an operational role in structuring, balancing, and refining the text.

Peer Validation & Initial Calibration

Before its wider release, the raw architectural draft was opened up to an intentional cohort of contributors and peer validators for iterative commenting and refinement. This collaborative feedback loop was critical for moving the text beyond abstract theory and grounding it in lived practice.

During this validation stage, peer contributors actively shaped the framework by:

  • Refining the Progression Descriptors: Grounding the developmental milestone descriptors (Emerging through Influential) to ensure they accurately reflect how systems leadership and learning goal progression manifest in real-world environments.
  • Expanding the Resource Index: Directly adding multiple field-tested peer-validated resources, core frameworks, and tools to enrich the Resource Maps.
  • Verifying Structural Relevance: Evaluating the clarity and utility of the elements to ensure the backbone remains flexible enough for formal, non-formal, and informal educational settings.

Open-Source Release & Continuous Community Expansion

Systems change cannot be fully captured by static or top-down academic synthesis; it requires continuous grounding in real-world application. Rather than treating this framework as a finished product, it is released directly into the world as an open-source resource.

The intention is to transition ownership of the framework's growth to the wider ecosystem of educators, community organizers, researchers, and systems practitioners. The document serves as a living, evolutionary backbone designed to be actively expanded by the global community.

Moving forward, practitioners are invited to directly contribute to and enrich this text by:

  • Writing and Expanding the Resource Library: Adding relevant local tools, localized methods, core frameworks, and key thinkers to broaden the lineage of each island.
  • Grounding through Lived Practice: Injecting field-based insights, case studies, and pedagogical experiences to ensure the learning outcomes remain practical and context-responsive.
  • Diversifying Worldviews: Contributing alternative framings, Global South methodologies, and land-based or Indigenous knowledge architectures to actively push the framework toward true pluriversality.

Through this decentralized, collective contribution model, the framework remains adaptive, dynamic, and directly accountable to the evolving needs of the field.
