Orientations

We offer these four orientations as a compass, and we hope you engage with them from where you stand.

Working with the orientations

These four values are a compass, not a checklist. A compass won't walk the path for you or name your destination; it tells you which way you're facing when the terrain gets confusing. Use the orientations the same way:

01

Consult them at junctions. When a decision is genuinely hard — who to involve, what to fund, when to push and when to wait — bring it back and ask which way each value points.

02

Apply them as ongoing inquiries. Resist treating them as badges to claim (we're just, we're plural). Instead ask, where is power concentrating here? Whose knowledge are we leaving out? Who actually decides? What are we taking, and what are we giving back?

03

Anticipate friction. The orientations will conflict with the demands of funders, deadlines, and institutions. They're here to help you notice and name those tensions, not to resolve them cleanly. Staying honest inside the contradiction is the work.

04

Prioritize the how alongside the what. A just outcome reached through an extractive process isn't aligned. These apply to how you meet, decide, and relate, not only to what you achieve.

05

Make them yours. Translate these values into your specific context, language, and systems of accountability. A living compass requires periodic recalibration.
